# 2026-08-12: `/impact/` rebuilt on the RSMF Phase 2 supporting evidence

Nothing pushed. Implements `impact-evidence-plan.md` in full. Four inputs were
put to the developer at the end of the first pass and three came back the same
day, so the second half of this log is those answers landing.

The input was the RSMF Phase 2 supporting-evidence deliverable, the optional
§12.1.2 upload. It had already done the research: every figure in it carries the
public link it came from, collected 2026-08-11. That is why this was a rebuild
and not a research task, and why the whole thing could be linked end to end.

## What the page gained

Three sections and a rebuilt aggregate band.

**`#beyond`, "Built on Overture, beyond OICR".** The tier `ia-proposal.md`
§ /impact/ Hub called the most persuasive evidence on the site, and which had
never rendered anywhere. Four organizations written up (AGARI/Africa CDC, Gen3,
Indoc/EBRAINS HealthDataCloud, Ferlab/CQDG) with the public source that
documents each dependency, then all ten as a table, then the two contributions
that came back the other way. AGARI leads: a continent-wide platform launched by
a continental agency in November 2025, whose procurement documents name the
framework it was built on, quoted rather than paraphrased.

**`#publications`, "Published work".** Five papers. Two of them are third-party,
which is the point of including them: an independent account of a national
portal built on Arranger, and a review of the field that cites Overture among
the platforms it surveys. The one under review is listed and labelled, not
linked, and the section says why.

**`#distribution`, "Distribution and release history".** npm across thirteen
packages, the container registries, documentation reach, and the seven
components' release history as a table.

**The aggregate band** went from four figures to six and every one of them now
links to what it was counted from. It was four, with only the grants count
linked.

Section order puts other people's work above ours, per the plan. The cards and
write-ups stay adjacent, since the cards are the link tree into them.

## Data

`data/dependents.ts`, `data/publications.ts`, `data/distribution.ts`, all new.
`metrics.ts` gained seven figures. `platforms.ts` gained `institution` and
`country`, and lost `adopters`: three unsourced entries nothing rendered,
superseded by ten sourced ones. `components.ts` gained `componentLabel()`, which
exists so the unshipped Control row prints as "Access and authorization (in
development)" rather than "TBD ()".

Platform cards now print their lead institution, country and the components they
run. Four of the six are led by someone other than us and the cards say so now.

## The rule, and its two exceptions

Every claim links to its source. Two cannot, and both say so in the sentence
that makes them rather than in a footnote: the Matomo documentation traffic,
which is our own analytics, and the GitHub code-search count, whose link GitHub
serves only to signed-in accounts. Being the page that flags its own two soft
claims is what the other thirty being hard is worth.

## `stableReleaseHistory` was wrong

Published as `8+ years` with no traceable source behind the eight. The git
histories put it at eleven, from Score's first tag on 2015-07-06. Corrected
rather than re-measured: nothing about it was a different measurement, it was an
understatement.

## Second pass: three of the four inputs answered

**All three portal URLs confirmed**, and all three verified live before going in:
`ohcrn.ca`, `genomelibrary.ca`, and `imicroseq-dataportal.ca`, the last keeping
its `/explorer` path from the old host (it redirects to `/explorer/clinical`).
That closes both `[NEEDS: URL]` items that had been open since the start of the
rebuild, and every card in tier 1 links to a live portal now. `VIRUSSEQ_PORTAL_LINK`
was renamed `IMICROSEQ_PORTAL_LINK` with the move: it was the last place on the
site still calling that platform VirusSeq, outside the `virusseq` anchor id,
which stays on purpose so existing fragment links keep landing.

**PCGL is launched.** Its submission portal is live and launching it was our own
directive, which settles the question `metrics.ts` had deliberately left open
rather than announce another institution's date. New `pcglLaunch`, the card is
dated like every other, its summary says the submission portal is live, and
`partnerLogos.ts` loses the one entry that had no start year to sort by.

**The ICGC-ARGO registered-user figure is gone**, on instruction rather than
reconciled. It was contested between two of the grant documents and was the only
tile in the aggregate band asserting something a reader could not check, so the
band is better without it. Removed from `metrics.ts` and from the ICGC-ARGO
write-up in `caseStudies.tsx`. npm downloads took the empty tile rather than the
band dropping to five: a tidier grid, and a figure the registry will confirm.
The value that was published is recorded in the comment left where the metric
was, so whoever resolves the disagreement knows what stood.

The fourth input, the three-row component-list disagreement, is still open and is
now written out row by row in `roadmap.md` § Inputs needed rather than summarized.
Working through it to answer the question properly turned up one useful pattern:
the evidence's own note says Stage is deployed wherever Arranger's front-end
components are used, and iMicroSeq and OHCRN both run Arranger, so the site may
simply be under-listing Stage rather than the two documents disagreeing about
anything real. Song and Score look like the reverse, with `caseStudies.tsx`
corroborating the site independently and the evidence document's own front matter
admitting its Song and Score counts are shaky.

## Checked

`npx tsc --noEmit` clean. `npm run build` succeeds with the same six pre-existing
broken-anchor warnings and no new ones: the `#beyond`, `#publications` and
`#distribution` links are bare hashes, which `components/Link.tsx` hands to the

Screenshotted at 1440 and 700 before calling any of it done. Two real problems
came out of that and are fixed: the two contribution links at the foot of
`#beyond` rendered as one continuous underlined run with only a space between
them, now separated by an "and"; and PCGL's row in the dependents table printed
"Lectern" bare, against the house style that never uses a codename alone.

`/products/` out at roughly 500px regardless of `--window-size`, so a 390px
capture looks like it overflows. It does not: both pages behave identically,
and at 700px the two tables scroll inside their own boxes as intended.
